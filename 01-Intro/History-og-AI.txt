## 1. What is Forward Deployed Engineering?

**Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE)** is an engineering approach where engineers work closely with customers or real-world users to understand their actual problems and build technical solutions for them.

A Forward Deployed Engineer is not only concerned with writing code. They need to understand the **problem, data, existing systems, AI/ML models, and the environment in which the solution will actually be used**.

### How FDE works

```text
Real-World Problem
        ↓
Understand the Problem
        ↓
Understand the Data
        ↓
Choose / Build a Solution
        ↓
Implement the Solution
        ↓
Deploy in Real Environment
        ↓
Observe Results
        ↓
Improve the Solution
```

### Main characteristics

* Works closely with users/customers.
* Understands the real-world problem before building.
* Combines software engineering with AI/ML when required.
* Works with existing systems and data.
* Focuses on practical deployment, not only theory.
* Continuously improves the solution based on real-world results.

The importance of FDE has increased with modern AI because organizations often need engineers who can take AI technology and **adapt it to a specific real-world problem**.

Human Intelligence

# 3. Origin of Artificial Intelligence

The idea of creating intelligent machines existed before modern AI, but the field of AI developed as computers became capable of performing increasingly complex calculations and researchers began asking whether machines could reproduce intelligent behavior.

Two extremely important people in the early history of AI are:

* **Alan Turing**
* **John McCarthy**

Later, researchers such as **Geoffrey Hinton** played an important role in the development of neural networks and modern deep learning.

---

# 4. Alan Turing

**Alan Turing** was a British mathematician, logician and computer scientist who made fundamental contributions to computer science.

He is especially important to AI because he asked an important question:

> Can machines think?

In 1950, Turing published a famous paper called **"Computing Machinery and Intelligence"**.

Instead of trying to define exactly what "thinking" means, Turing proposed a way of evaluating machine intelligence through conversation.

This became known as the:

### Turing Test

The basic idea is:

```text
Human
  |
  | conversation
  ↓
Machine

Can the evaluator distinguish
the machine from a human?
```

Turing's work became one of the important foundations for thinking about machine intelligence.

---

# 5. John McCarthy and the Birth of AI

**John McCarthy** was an American computer scientist and one of the pioneers of Artificial Intelligence.

He is widely credited with introducing the term:

**Artificial Intelligence**

### Dartmouth Workshop — 1956

In 1956, John McCarthy and other researchers organized the **Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence**.

This event is considered an important milestone in the formal beginning of AI as a research field.

The early researchers believed that aspects of intelligence could potentially be described precisely enough for machines to reproduce them.

This led to early work on:

* Logic
* Reasoning
* Problem solving
* Search
* Knowledge representation
* Rule-based systems

# 6. Rule-Based AI

One of the earliest approaches to AI was **rule-based AI**.

In this approach, humans explicitly provide rules to the computer.

The basic structure is:

```text
IF condition is true
        ↓
THEN perform an action
```

### Example

```text
IF temperature > 38°C
        ↓
THEN fever = true
```

The machine does not discover this rule itself.

A human has already written it.

---

# 7. Rule-Based System

A rule-based system generally contains:

* **Rules** — instructions written by humans.
* **Facts** — information provided to the system.
* **Inference mechanism** — applies rules to the available facts.

### Example

Suppose:

```text
Fact:
Temperature = 39°C

Rule:
IF temperature > 38°C
THEN fever = true
```

The system checks the rule:

```text
39 > 38
   ↓
TRUE
   ↓
fever = true
```

### Flow

```text
Input / Facts
      ↓
Check Rules
      ↓
Apply Matching Rule
      ↓
Generate Result
```

---

# 8. Limitations of Rule-Based AI

Rule-based systems work well when the problem is simple and clearly defined.

However, real-world problems can contain:

* Huge numbers of possibilities
* Exceptions
* Uncertainty
* Ambiguous language
* Changing situations
* Complex relationships

For example, imagine trying to understand every possible sentence using manually written rules.

You would need an enormous number of rules.

Therefore, instead of manually writing every possible rule, researchers increasingly explored systems that could **learn patterns from data**.

This led to Machine Learning.

---

# 9. Machine Learning

**Machine Learning (ML)** is an approach where computers learn patterns from data instead of depending entirely on manually written rules.

### Traditional programming

```text
Rules + Data
     ↓
   Output
```

### Machine Learning

```text
Data + Examples
      ↓
Learning Algorithm
      ↓
Learned Model
      ↓
Prediction
```

The important difference is:

**In rule-based systems, humans explicitly write the rules.**

**In machine learning, the algorithm learns useful patterns from examples.**

# 10. Classification in Machine Learning

One common machine-learning task is **classification**.

Classification means predicting which category something belongs to.

### Example

Suppose we have emails:

```text
Email 1 → Spam
Email 2 → Not Spam
Email 3 → Spam
Email 4 → Not Spam
```

The machine-learning algorithm learns patterns from these examples.

Later:

```text
New Email
    ↓
ML Model
    ↓
Spam / Not Spam
```

Classification produces a **category**, not normally a continuous numerical value.

Examples:

* Spam / Not Spam
* Cat / Dog

# 12. Statistical Models

Before modern deep learning became dominant, many AI and ML systems relied heavily on **statistics and probability**.

A statistical model attempts to represent relationships in data using mathematical and probabilistic concepts.

Instead of always saying:

```text
This answer is definitely correct.
```

a statistical model can work with probabilities:

```text
Answer A → 70%
Answer B → 20%
Answer C → 10%
```

The fundamental idea is:

**Use data to estimate the probability of possible outcomes.**

# 13. N-Gram Language Models

A language model attempts to predict or estimate what words are likely to appear in a sequence.

N-gram models do this using groups of words.

### Unigram

A **unigram** considers one word at a time.

```text
I
am
learning
Java
```

It mainly considers the individual probability of each word.

### Bigram

A **bigram** considers two words.

```text
I am
am learning
learning Java
```

For example:

```text
P(Java | learning)
```

means the probability of "Java" given the previous word "learning".

### Trigram

A **trigram** considers three words.

```text
I am learning
am learning Java
```

### General idea

```text
Unigram → 1 word
Bigram  → 2 words
Trigram → 3 words
N-gram  → N words
```

---

# 14. Limitation of N-Gram Models

The major limitation of n-gram models is that they have **limited context**.

Consider:

```text
The bank was crowded.
```

and:

```text
I sat near the river bank.
```

The word "bank" can have different meanings depending on context.

A small n-gram may not have enough information to understand the relationship between words that are far apart.

This created the need for better methods of handling sequences and context.

That led toward neural networks and eventually RNNs, LSTMs and Transformers.

# 20. Sequence Processing

Not all data is like an image.

In many types of data, **order matters**.

Examples:

* Sentences
* Speech
* Time-series data
* Sequences of events

Consider:

```text
Dog bites man.
```

and:

```text
Man bites dog.
```

The same words are present, but the meaning changes because their order changes.

Therefore, a model processing language needs to consider:

**What came before?**

This is called **sequence processing**.

# 21. RNN — Recurrent Neural Network

**RNN stands for Recurrent Neural Network.**

RNNs were designed to process sequential data.

The important idea is that information from previous steps can influence the current step.

### Basic idea

```text
Input 1 → RNN → Hidden State 1
                    ↓
Input 2 → RNN → Hidden State 2
                    ↓
Input 3 → RNN → Hidden State 3
```

The hidden state carries information from previous parts of the sequence.

Therefore:

```text
Previous Information
       +
Current Input
       ↓
      RNN
       ↓
Updated Information
```

---

# 22. Why RNNs Were Useful for Language

Consider:

```text
I grew up in India and I speak ______.
```

To predict the missing word, information from earlier words can be useful.

RNNs were designed to maintain information from previous steps.

This made them useful for:

* Language
* Speech
* Time series
* Sequence prediction

However, RNNs had difficulties with very long sequences.

# 23. LSTM

**LSTM stands for Long Short-Term Memory.**

LSTM is a special type of recurrent neural network designed to handle long-term dependencies better than basic RNNs.

LSTMs use gates to control information.

Important gates include:

* Forget gate
* Input gate
* Output gate

### Simplified idea

```text
Previous Information
        ↓
   ┌───────────┐
   │   LSTM    │
   │           │
   │ Forget    │
   │ Input     │
   │ Output    │
   └───────────┘
        ↓
Updated Memory
```

The purpose is to decide:

* What information should be forgotten?
* What new information should be stored?
* What information should be used as output?

# 28. The Problem With Sequential Models

RNNs and LSTMs process sequences step by step.

For a long sentence:

```text
Word 1 → Word 2 → Word 3 → Word 4 → ... → Word 100
```

Information has to travel through many steps.

This makes it harder for the model to directly connect distant words.

Researchers therefore looked for a better mechanism.

The key idea became:

**Attention.**

---

# 29. Attention

Attention allows a model to determine which parts of the input are important when processing a particular part.

Instead of only relying on information passed sequentially:

```text
Word 1 → Word 2 → Word 3 → Word 4
```

attention allows a word to consider other relevant words.

### Basic idea

```text
                 Sentence
                    ↓
          +---------+---------+
          ↓         ↓         ↓
        Word 1    Word 2    Word 3
          ↖         ↑         ↗
             Attention
```

The model can assign different importance to different words.

---

# 30. Example of Attention

Consider:

```text
The animal didn't cross the road because it was tired.
```

When processing:

```text
it
```

the model needs to understand which earlier word "it" refers to.

Attention allows the model to consider the other words and determine which ones are more relevant.

The central idea is:

**Not every word is equally important for understanding every other word.**

# 33. Self-Attention

Self-attention means that elements of a sequence can consider other elements of the **same sequence** when creating their representations.

For example:

```text
The cat sat on the mat because it was tired.
```

When processing:

```text
it
```

the model can pay attention to other words in the sentence to determine which information is relevant.

This gives the model a powerful way to capture relationships between words.

---

# 34. Query, Key and Value

Self-attention uses three important concepts:

* **Query (Q)**
* **Key (K)**
* **Value (V)**

A simple way to understand them:

### Query

"What information am I looking for?"

### Key

"What type of information do I contain?"

### Value

"What information should I provide?"

The attention mechanism compares queries with keys to determine how strongly different pieces of information should contribute.

The mathematical form is:

```text
Attention(Q,K,V)
=
softmax(QKᵀ / √dₖ)V
```

For now, the most important thing to remember is:

**Attention calculates how much importance one part of the sequence should give to another part.**

---

# 35. Multi-Head Attention

Transformers don't necessarily use only one attention operation.

They can use multiple attention heads.

Each head can learn different relationships.

Conceptually:

```text
                Input
                  ↓
        +---------+---------+
        ↓         ↓         ↓
     Head 1     Head 2     Head 3
        ↓         ↓         ↓
        +---------+---------+
                  ↓
               Combined
                  ↓
                Output
```

This allows the model to learn different types of relationships simultaneously.

# 36. Why Transformers Were Important

Transformers solved several limitations of previous sequence-processing approaches.

Important advantages include:

* Strong handling of relationships between distant words.
* Attention allows direct interaction between sequence elements.
* Training can be highly parallelized.
* They scale effectively with large amounts of data and computation.
* They became the foundation of many modern language models.

This eventually led to the development of very large Transformer-based models.
